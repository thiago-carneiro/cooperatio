# Review checklist

A checklist for the reviewer of the guide *Guide to preparing a production cell*. Work through the groups in order; the first three decide whether the guide can be reviewed at all, the rest decide whether it passes. Every item says what to verify and how. Items marked **blocking** are reasons to stop and return the guide; the others are findings to record even when the verdict would still be `accepted`.

## Before reading the guide

- **Brief present and followed (blocking).** Open the brief and list its demands: target, audience, the three non-negotiables, the owner decision, the stopping criterion. The guide was written for a teammate who has read the principle once and has never prepared a cell. Check the guide's opening against that audience: does it say what a cell is and what preparing one means without assuming the reader has done it? A guide written for an expert who needs a refresher, or for someone who has not read the principle, fails this item.
- **Length (blocking).** The brief sets 2,500 to 3,500 words. Count the guide's words as the validator would: the body of the Markdown, not the title. Record the count. Below 2,500 the guide is a note, not a guide; above 3,500 it was not cut.
- **Form.** The brief's `nn-form`: plain Markdown, no HTML, no absolute path, no authoring date, no machine name. Search the guide for `<`, for `/` at the start of a path, for a date-shaped string, for a hostname. Version tags such as `v1` and artifact names are allowed; a date of making is not (principle, golden rule 9).
- **Canon carried (blocking).** The canon (`02-canon.json`) is the single source of numbers, names, terms and rules for this guide. Check that every number, name, term and rule the guide states is in the canon or follows from it. A number that appears in the guide and nowhere in the canon is a canon defect, not a writing defect: route it as a note against the canon section, and do not accept the guide until it is resolved. The canon's sections are still `pending` at the time of this review; a number used in the guide without a canon entry is blocking regardless.

## Sources of truth and marks

- **Every statement about the method (blocking).** `nn-source` says every method statement can be checked in the principle. Take the guide paragraph by paragraph; for each claim about how the method works, find the sentence in the principle that says it. Where the guide adds a detail the principle does not have, that is a method claim the guide invented: either the detail is in the principle and the guide must cite it, or it is not and the guide must drop it or mark it as the guide's own advice, clearly outside the method.
- **Every statement about a registered prior (blocking).** The principle registers one prior, `004-adoption-guide`, with four recorded facts: 1054 words at the close, the size range 900 to 1400, and the lowest dimension score 4 in its one review. Any sentence in the guide that touches that prior must carry the `[prior:004-adoption-guide]` mark and must agree with the recorded fact. Read every sentence containing "reference case", "prior instance", "of the case" or "the executed instance" — the canon's `prior-statement` triggers — and check it. A mark that is present but points at the wrong fact is as bad as a missing mark.
- **Numbers from the canon, not from prose.** The recorded fact 1054 is the guide's word count at the close, not a limit any new guide inherits (principle, golden rule 2). If the guide uses 1054 as a target or as an example of a good length, it has misread the fact. The same for 900 to 1400: that is the range of the prior's brief, not a rule for every guide.
- **No efficacy claim (blocking).** The principle's `efficacy` triggers list the phrasings that turn a description into a claim — "the method found", "the method caught", "the gates caught", "the review found", "pays off", "the only way", "observed in practice", and the rest. Search the guide for each. The guide is a how-to, not evidence; a sentence that says the method catches errors, or that the gates prevent a class of defect, is a claim the material cannot support (the principle's section 9 calls the evidence one-armed). Rewrite as "the method is designed to", "the gate measures", "the review is meant to", not as a finding.
- **No claim about who caught what (blocking, in particular).** The one execution's review found the blocker in the first round of the reflexive application (principle, lessons), but the guide must not turn that into "the review catches the blocker". The guide may say that the method puts a reviewer who did not produce in front of the product; it must not say the review found, or would find, anything.
- **Scope declared, not implied.** The principle is explicit that the method has one executed instance, documentary, and that the inter-domain instances carry the design, not a result. If the guide's example or aside implies the method has been run on a physical or software target, cut it or correct it.

## Terms of the canon, in the canon's sense

- **Names and terms (blocking).** `nn-terms`: the terms of the canon are used in the canon's sense. The canon's `names and terms` array is the list to check against, with its `prefer` spelling and its gloss marker. For each term the guide uses, find the canon entry and compare: same spelling, same meaning. A term used in a sense the canon does not carry — because the writer meant something adjacent — is a finding, and a blocking one if the term is load-bearing in a section.
- **Terms to avoid (blocking).** The canon lists terms to avoid: "quality gate", "checkpoint", "pipeline", "feedback", "iteration", "single source of truth", "bible", "ruler", "review board". Search the guide for each, in every inflection. These are the words a newcomer brings with them from other methods; the guide's job is to replace them with the canon's vocabulary, and using one is exactly the failure the canon exists to prevent. The canon's own term for the single source is "canon", for the check on delivered quality is "gate" or "gate with magnitude", for the loop is "round".
- **The canon's own terms are the ones the guide must teach.** The guide is a newcomer's first contact with the vocabulary. Check that it introduces artifact, artifact graph, role card, contract, gate, note, canon, validator, independent review, rubric, stopping criterion, and round, and that it uses each with the canon's meaning. A newcomer who closes the guide with a different sense of "gate" than the canon's will misread the next document.
- **The human decision is in the canon, not invented.** The canon's rules section carries the decisions the guide must not contradict. If the guide presents a decision as the owner's — the audience, the scope — check it against the canon's rules; a decision the guide invents for the owner is a finding (principle, golden rule 7).
- **Origin labels.** The canon's `origin labels` are `[method]`, `[brief]`, `[hypothesis]`. Statements the guide makes about what the method is must be traceable to the method; statements about this target to the brief; statements about the material's confidence to the hypothesis label. Check that the guide does not present a hypothesis as a method fact, and that a sentence about how the method is *meant* to work is not written as though the material has shown it.

## Structure and completeness for a newcomer

- **The brief, first.** A newcomer's first question is what the cell is for. Check that the guide opens with the target, the audience and the stopping criterion, in the canon's vocabulary, before it describes any tool. A guide that opens with the artifact graph has put the method before the reason for it.
- **The sources of truth.** Check that the guide says, per content type, where the truth lives: facts from the corpus or the primary source, numbers from the canon or from the instrument that measured them, exact text from the original (principle, golden rule 2). A guide that says "the writer" without naming the source per type has dropped the rule the newcomer most needs.
- **The canon.** Check that the guide presents the canon as the canon does: one source for numbers, names, terms and decisions, with an owner and a version per section, and with the terms to avoid. A newcomer who does not know the canon has an owner will treat it as a dictionary, and the next divergence of a number goes unnoticed.
- **The artifact graph.** Check that the guide says what the graph is made of — typed files, each with an owner, schema, dependencies and hash — and that it warns, as the principle does, that a graph with a cycle is wrong. If the guide draws an example graph, check it is acyclic and that each node has an owner; an example that cannot be drawn without a cycle contradicts the method.
- **The eleven components.** The principle names eleven things that do not change from domain to domain: artifact graph, role cards, contracts, gates with magnitude, routed notes, canon, validator, independent review with a fixed rubric, instrument fixed at the root, stopping criterion, gate sensitivity test. Check that the guide names all eleven, and that it does not quietly rename one. A guide that teaches ten is incomplete; one that teaches twelve has invented a component.
- **The role card fields.** The canon's `role card fields` are the card's fields: Decides, Does not decide, Inputs, Outputs, Exit gate, Notes it emits, Code-conversion degree. If the guide presents a role card or its template, check that every field appears and that the "does not decide" field is explained — it is the field that returns a defect to its owner, and a newcomer who skips it will compensate for another role's defect (principle, component table).
- **The recipe.** The principle's recipe has twelve steps, ending with the case method document. If the guide claims to tell a newcomer how to prepare a cell, check it walks the recipe in order and does not skip the expensive or unpopular steps: the initial canon, the role cards, the gates with their limit justifications, the rubric written before the first review, and the declared stop. A guide that skips the rubric-before-review step produces rounds the newcomer cannot compare.
